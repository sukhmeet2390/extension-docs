Screen sharing is usually a last-minute action. Someone asks for a demo, you open the right browser window, and you click Share before thinking about everything else inside it. WhatsApp Web makes that risky because the visible page includes more than the message you meant to discuss.

A good privacy check does not need to be complicated. You want a short routine that catches names, previews, profile photos, media, drafts, and the actual sharing target before another person can see them.

## The five-minute WhatsApp Web check

Use the checklist below before a meeting, sales demo, support call, webinar, or recording. You do not have to hide every part of WhatsApp every time. The goal is to make a deliberate choice instead of sharing the default page by accident.

### 1. Open the correct WhatsApp Web page

Start with [web.whatsapp.com](https://web.whatsapp.com/) in Chrome and let the page finish loading. If you have several windows open, identify the one connected to the account you intend to use.

Do not assume that a tab title is enough. Look at the page itself. Confirm that you are in the browser tab you expect, that the correct chat is open, and that no sign-in or connection screen is going to interrupt your presentation.

If you use Privacy Blur, the floating widget appears on WhatsApp Web. Open it before the meeting starts. The extension works locally on WhatsApp Web; it does not modify other websites or hide anything on your desktop.

### 2. Turn on a safe starting state

If you are not sure which parts of WhatsApp will be visible, turn on the global blur toggle first. This gives you a broad visual privacy layer while you decide which zones to leave readable.

Then inspect the page. A broad blur is useful for a rushed meeting, but you may prefer selective controls for a tutorial where viewers need to understand the interface. Privacy Blur has independent zones for messages, contact names, chat-list names, preview text, profile photos, the compose box, images and videos, and Status or About information.

![WhatsApp Web fully blurred with the global Privacy Blur state enabled](https://amrita-labs.com/assets/blog/whatsapp-blur/whatsapp-full-blurred.png)

### 3. Check the chat list

The sidebar can reveal a surprising amount of context. Look for:

- Private names and group names
- Last-message previews
- Profile photos
- Project or customer references
- Unread counts and timestamps

If viewers only need to see the main workflow, blur the chat-list names and previews. If the list is part of your explanation, keep the structure visible but hide the identifying text. Do not leave the sidebar unchecked just because your main conversation is safe.

### 4. Check the active conversation header

Read the contact or group name at the top of the active conversation. Then check the avatar beside it. These details can identify a private chat even when the message area is covered.

Enable contact-name blur and profile-photo blur when the identity of the person or group is not relevant to the meeting. This is especially important for client demos, recorded tutorials, and public webinars, where the audience may not know which information was supposed to remain private.

### 5. Check message text and timestamps

Message blur is the obvious step, but do it deliberately. Scroll through the active conversation and check both recent and older message rows. A single readable sentence may reveal a name, phone number, address, price, or private plan.

Also look at timestamps and message status indicators. They may not contain the message itself, but they can still provide context about a person or conversation. You do not need to remove every visual element for every call; you do need to know what remains visible.

### 6. Check previews and search fields

The chat list is not the only place short text appears. Search fields, link previews, quoted replies, and notification-like rows can expose words you were not planning to show.

Before sharing, clear a private search query and blur preview text if the sidebar is visible. If you typed a contact name to find the right conversation, do not leave that name sitting in the search box while you present.

### 7. Check media and profile photos

A photo can identify someone faster than a message. So can a document thumbnail, video frame, company logo, or familiar group avatar.

Look through the active chat for images, videos, files, and media previews. If the meeting does not need them, enable the Images & Videos zone. Add profile-photo blur for avatars in the sidebar, header, and chat information view.

![WhatsApp Web messages and media blurred while the Privacy Blur panel stays available for local controls](https://amrita-labs.com/assets/blog/whatsapp-blur/whatsapp-messages-blurred.png)

### 8. Check the compose box

Draft text is easy to forget because it is not part of the conversation history. If you typed a reply before the call, the compose box may contain the most sensitive line on the page.

Clear the draft or enable compose-box blur. Do this again if you type while sharing. A demonstration can turn into an accidental disclosure when someone sees a private sentence before you send it.

### 9. Check Status and About details

Contact information can remain visible outside the main message area. If you open a chat information drawer, look for Status or About text, phone numbers, and other details that viewers might not need.

Privacy Blur includes a Status / About zone for this kind of content. If the view is route-dependent or not currently open, simply close the drawer or avoid opening it during the meeting. The safest surface is the one you have actually inspected.

### 10. Choose the right sharing target

Chrome’s [screen-sharing guidance](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/avoiding-oversharing-when-screen-sharing/) explains why sharing a tab usually exposes less unrelated information than sharing the entire screen. When your meeting tool allows it, share the specific WhatsApp Web tab.

If tab sharing is not available, use a browser window containing only the intended work. Avoid sharing the whole desktop unless you need to demonstrate something outside the browser. A full-screen share can expose notifications, other applications, bookmarks, and unrelated tabs.

Remember that tab selection and content blur are separate checks. Choosing the right tab does not hide a readable WhatsApp message. Blurring WhatsApp does not protect a private document in another window.

## Match the blur to the meeting

For a team standup, selective blur can keep navigation readable while hiding names, previews, and profile photos. For a client demo, sales call, webinar, or recording, start with the global blur state, use a test conversation or dummy account, and reveal only what supports the explanation. Support calls deserve the broad state while you move between chats because each new header, preview, and media item needs another check.

Selective zones are useful when the interface itself is part of the lesson: hide names while showing buttons, hide previews while demonstrating the list, or hide the compose box before typing. The global toggle is the safer starting point when the meeting is rushed or you are unsure which zones are active. It is not a guarantee; inspect the sidebar, header, media, compose box, and open information drawers before sharing.

## Keep checking after you start sharing

The privacy pass is not finished when the call begins. WhatsApp Web can update as messages arrive, chats move in the list, and you open new views. Keep blur enabled, avoid opening unrelated pages, and look at the shared surface after navigation.

If you use hover reveal, remember that it is for your local view. Reveal only what you need, keep the pointer over that item, and move away when you are done. Do not turn off the whole privacy layer just to read one line.

## Know the limits of visual privacy

Privacy Blur is a visual privacy tool. Its blur operation runs locally on WhatsApp Web, and no WhatsApp message content is sent to Amrita Labs. The extension does not send message text, contact names, phone numbers, photos, videos, or chat files to Amrita Labs for blurring. Anonymous product-usage analytics are separate from WhatsApp content.

Visual blur is not encryption. It does not protect the underlying page from browser developer tools, and it does not hide content in another tab, application, device, or desktop surface. It cannot guarantee that a screenshot or recording contains no private information. Choose the shared surface carefully and review what you show.
