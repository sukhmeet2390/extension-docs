7 Best Pinterest Tools for Creators: Scoring, Research, Scheduling, and Analytics

2026-11-16
#pinterest#pinterest-tools#pinterest-scheduling#pinterest-analytics#creator-tools#pinello

The best Pinterest tool depends on the job you need to do.

A creator checking a draft before publishing needs something different from a creator scheduling a month of content. Someone measuring outbound clicks needs a reporting tool, while someone collecting ideas may only need Pinterest’s own boards and Trends experience.

This list covers seven tools across those jobs. It does not treat them as interchangeable, and it does not rank them by a made-up score. Features, limits, and prices can change, so use the linked official pages to confirm the current offer before subscribing.

Pinello is a free Chrome extension that scores Pinterest pins across 5 dimensions — image, board fit, title, description, and hashtags — in real time.

1. Pinterest Analytics and Creator Hub: measure your own results

Start with Pinterest’s own tools if your question is about published performance.

Pinterest’s creator guidance describes Analytics as a place to review your overall presence, top Pins and boards, audience information, and performance over time. Pin stats can show interactions on an individual Pin. Depending on the goal, useful metrics may include impressions, saves, Pin clicks, outbound clicks, total audience, engaged audience, and video viewing fields.

Creator Hub is the mobile-oriented home for creator tools and analytics, while the desktop experience can expose Analytics Overview. Pinterest says a business account is required for the full Creator Hub analytics workflow. Some account and website reporting features also have prerequisites, so do not assume that a public Pin’s visible activity equals your complete account report.

Best for: measuring what happened after you published.

Watch for: Analytics is not a pre-publish quality checker. It can show that a Pin received clicks or saves, but it does not rewrite a weak title before publication and it does not prove which creative choice caused a result.

Official pages: Pinterest analytics explained and Creator Hub.

2. Pinterest Trends: research search interest

Pinterest Trends is useful when your question is about what people are searching for or how interest changes over time. The official Trends site lets you explore terms and compare topics where data is available.

Use it before designing a batch of Pins, not as a guarantee that a topic will produce traffic. Search interest can inform your wording and seasonality research, but it does not replace checking whether a topic fits your audience, product, or destination.

Trends also should not be confused with a Pin quality score. A topic can have interest while your individual Pin is unclear. Conversely, a carefully targeted niche topic may be valuable even if it is not the largest visible trend.

Best for: topic exploration and planning questions.

Watch for: not every query will have the same coverage, and a trend line does not tell you that a particular image or title will perform.

Official page: Pinterest Trends.

3. Pinello: improve a Pin before publishing

Pinello is for the moment before the publish button, when a small edit is still available.

Its Feed Score appears while you browse and helps you decide which Pin deserves a closer look. Pin Detail Score can use fuller information after you open a Pin. In Create Pin, Pin Doctor checks five areas: image, board fit, title, description, and hashtags or topics.

The workflow is intentionally narrower than a scheduling or analytics platform. It focuses on a draft’s clarity and context, not account reporting. The score is a Pinello signal based on available data. It is not Pinterest’s ranking score and does not predict reach, saves, clicks, sales, or virality.

Pinello also includes local Favorites, sorting and filtering, SEO Audit, Board Match, and Pro CSV export for scored Pins and Favorites. AI actions are user-triggered and copy-only: Pinello can suggest text and put it on the clipboard, but it does not type into Pinterest or publish for you.

Best for: pre-publish scoring and a browser-side second opinion.

Watch for: it is not an analytics dashboard, scheduler, keyword-volume database, or reach forecast.

Official page: Pinello.

4. Tailwind: schedule and plan Pinterest content

Tailwind is a Pinterest-focused marketing platform with creation, scheduling, keyword, and engagement products. Its current pricing page presents mix-and-match options and a bundle, with plans priced per Pinterest account. The page lists separate areas for Pin scheduling and creation, Pinterest SEO, and Pinterest engagement, plus a free starting option.

Tailwind’s Pinterest pages describe visual scheduling, SmartSchedule, spacing rules, Pin creation, copywriting assistance, and keyword tools. Those features make it a candidate for creators who want a repeat publishing workflow rather than a one-off post.

The official pricing page currently displays annual prices of $17.99 per month for Pin Scheduling & Creation, $11.99 per month for Pinterest SEO, and $11.99 per month for Pinterest Engagement, with a displayed bundle price of $29.98 per month when billed annually. Prices and packaging are time-sensitive; verify the page for your account and billing cycle before purchase.

Best for: creators who want Pinterest-specific scheduling and planning tools.

Watch for: scheduling and keyword research do not replace a final human review of the Pin, destination, and brand voice. Treat any “best time” or engagement language as a planning feature, not a promise.

Official pages: Tailwind for Pinterest and Tailwind pricing.

5. Buffer: schedule across channels

Buffer is a broader social publishing platform that includes Pinterest. Its Pinterest page describes scheduling image and video Pins, cross-posting, organizing ideas, and viewing metrics such as saves, impressions, and engagement rate.

Buffer’s pricing page currently shows a free plan with up to three channels and ten scheduled posts per channel. Its paid Essentials plan is displayed at $5 per month for one channel when billed yearly, and Team at $10 per month for one channel when billed yearly. The same page describes a 14-day trial for paid plans. Because channel counts, billing cycles, and regional taxes affect the final price, confirm the official pricing page before relying on those figures.

Buffer is a practical choice when Pinterest sits beside Instagram, Facebook, TikTok, or other channels and you want one planning interface. It is less specialized than a Pinterest-only research or scoring tool.

Best for: cross-channel scheduling and a shared content calendar.

Watch for: scheduling consistency does not prove that a Pin will earn traffic. Use Pinterest Analytics and your site analytics for outcomes.

Official pages: Buffer for Pinterest and Buffer pricing.

6. Later: manage Pinterest with a wider social calendar

Later supports Pinterest as one of the social profiles in its broader scheduling platform. Its pricing page currently lists a Starter plan with one social set containing eight profiles, including Pinterest, and up to 30 scheduled posts per profile. The page also lists platform analytics and AI content tools for that plan. Higher tiers add more profiles, collaboration, approvals, and longer analytics history.

The displayed Starter price at the time of review was $18.75 USD per month when billed yearly, with the page showing a promotional three-month-free message. Promotions and plan details can change, so treat that figure as a snapshot rather than a permanent quote.

Later may fit a creator or small team that needs Pinterest inside a visual social workflow. It is not primarily a Pin-quality checker. If your main problem is unclear board fit or a weak description, a calendar alone will not catch it.

Best for: multi-platform planning with visual scheduling and analytics.

Watch for: the number of social sets and profiles matters more than a headline monthly price. Check what Pinterest access is included in your selected plan.

Official page: Later pricing.

7. Metricool: scheduling and reporting across brands

Metricool combines social scheduling, analytics, competitor-profile analysis, and reporting. Its pricing page currently lists a free plan with one brand, up to 20 posts per month, analysis of five competitor profiles, and 30 days of analytics. Paid Starter pricing is displayed from $20 per month in the USD view, with higher brand limits and additional reporting features.

This makes Metricool worth considering for creators who manage more than one brand or want reporting alongside publishing. The exact value depends on how many brands, profiles, and history you need. The official page also lists integrations and add-ons, so read the current plan details rather than assuming every feature is included at every tier.

Best for: multi-brand scheduling, reports, and cross-network measurement.

Watch for: competitor comparisons are context, not proof that another account’s result can be reproduced. Keep your own goals and Pinterest Analytics in the loop.

Official page: Metricool pricing.

Which tool should you choose?

Choose by the stage of your workflow:

Your main job Start with
Improve a draft before publishing Pinello
Measure published Pins and account activity Pinterest Analytics and Creator Hub
Explore search interest Pinterest Trends
Schedule Pinterest-first content Tailwind
Schedule Pinterest with other channels Buffer or Later
Manage several brands and reports Metricool

Many creators will use more than one. That is not unnecessary duplication when the tools answer different questions. Pinello can help you review a draft. A scheduler can queue approved content. Pinterest Analytics can measure the result. Trends can inform the next topic.

Keep the claims honest

No tool in this list can promise a ranking position, a specific number of saves, or viral distribution. A research trend is not a result. A scheduler is not an audience. A score is not analytics. Analytics is not a causal explanation.

The most reliable workflow is still human:

  1. Research a topic and audience.
  2. Create the image and copy.
  3. Check the draft before publishing.
  4. Schedule or publish it through a tool that fits your workflow.
  5. Measure your own results after enough time has passed.
  6. Use what you learned to make the next draft clearer.

If the gap in your workflow is the pre-publish review, try Pinello. It is designed to help you improve the Pin you control, without pretending to predict what Pinterest will do afterward.
