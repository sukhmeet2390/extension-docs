Pinterest Pin Scoring vs Pinterest Analytics: Improve Before Publishing or Measure After?

2026-11-16
#pinello#pinterest#pinterest-analytics#pinterest-pin-scoring#creator-tools

Pinterest Pin scoring and Pinterest Analytics are easy to put in the same bucket. Both show information about Pins, both can influence what you do next, and both are often discussed as if they answer one question: “Will this Pin perform?”

They do not answer that question in the same way.

Scoring is a pre-publish inspection. It helps you review the parts of a draft you can still change. Analytics is a post-publish measurement system. It helps you understand what happened to your own content after people had a chance to see and interact with it.

The useful workflow is not scoring instead of analytics. It is scoring before publishing, then analytics after publishing, with a clear boundary between a quality check and an observed result.

Pinello is a free Chrome extension that scores Pinterest pins across 5 dimensions — image, board fit, title, description, and hashtags — in real time.

What Pinterest Pin scoring is for

A Pin score is most useful when the Pin is still in front of you and a change is inexpensive.

You can inspect whether the image communicates the subject at a glance, whether the selected board is a reasonable home for the idea, and whether the title and description explain what someone will find after the click. You can also review whether the hashtags or topics support the same subject rather than pulling the draft in several directions.

That is a quality-control job. It is closer to proofreading a product page than to reading a financial report.

Pinello has a quick Feed Score while you browse and a Pin Detail Score when more closeup information is available. The score can differ between those surfaces because the available fields differ. A feed card may provide a useful first signal; the closeup can provide fuller title, description, board, image, creator, and Pin information.

The score is not Pinterest’s official ranking score. It does not predict impressions, saves, outbound clicks, sales, or virality. It is a structured way to notice gaps before you publish.

What Pinterest Analytics is for

Pinterest’s own analytics tools answer a different question: what did your account and Pins receive over a selected period?

Pinterest’s creator guidance describes metrics such as impressions, saves, Pin clicks, outbound clicks, total audience, and engaged audience. For video Pins, the available view and watch-time fields can add more context. The metric that matters depends on the goal. Awareness may call for impressions and audience measures. Traffic work may focus more on outbound clicks. A conversion workflow needs tracking that connects Pinterest activity with an outcome.

Business account users can access analytics from the Analytics Overview area or Creator Hub, depending on the surface. Pin stats can also show interactions for an individual Pin. Pinterest’s organic reporting documentation explains that account and Pin analytics have defined look-back windows and prerequisites, including business-account access for some analytics and website-claiming considerations.

Those details matter because Analytics is not a universal score for all Pinterest content. It is a report about a particular account, Pin, audience, date range, and measurement setup.

If a Pin has a high save count, Analytics can tell you that the save activity occurred. It cannot, by itself, prove which single design choice caused it. The Pin may have benefited from the topic, the account, seasonality, distribution, the destination, or several factors at once.

The difference in one table

Question Pin scoring Pinterest Analytics
When is it most useful? Before publishing or while revising After publishing and collecting data
What does it inspect? Draft fields and available Pin context Observed account or Pin activity
Who controls the inputs? The creator can revise them The creator measures what happened
What can it support? A clearer next edit A better-informed review of past performance
What can it not guarantee? Reach, ranking, or virality Causation or future performance

The distinction prevents a common mistake: treating a pre-publish score as a forecast, or treating a post-publish metric as a recipe that will work identically next time.

A practical before-and-after workflow

Start with the draft. Open Create Pin and review the image, board, title, description, and hashtags or topics. If Pinello identifies a focus issue, begin there rather than rewriting every field at once.

For example, the image may be attractive but hard to understand at thumbnail size. The board may be related but too broad. The title may say “ideas” without identifying the audience or subject. The description may repeat the title without explaining the destination. These are useful observations because you can address them immediately.

Make the edit yourself. If you request an AI suggestion in Pinello, the action is user-triggered and copy-only. Pinello can place a proposed title, description, or topic set on the clipboard, but it does not type into Pinterest fields or publish the Pin. Read the result for accuracy, tone, and claims before deciding whether to paste it.

Then review the revised draft. A stronger score means the draft satisfies more of Pinello’s checks with the data available. It does not mean Pinterest has promised to distribute it.

After publishing, switch tools. Use Pinterest Analytics or Pin stats to record the metrics that match your goal. If the goal is traffic, pay attention to outbound clicks and the destination experience. If the goal is saving ideas for later, saves may be useful context. If the goal is conversion, use the tracking setup required for that measurement rather than guessing from a visible Pin count.

Why analytics cannot replace a pre-publish check

Analytics arrives after the decision. It can show that a Pin received few outbound clicks, but it cannot change the title before that Pin is published. It can show that a Pin was saved often, but it may not tell you whether the image, topic, board, or account context deserves the credit.

Without a pre-publish routine, creators often make the same preventable mistakes repeatedly: an empty description, a board that is only loosely related, unreadable image text, or a title that makes the topic unclear. Analytics may eventually reveal a pattern, but the cost of learning is another published draft.

A scoring pass is not a replacement for learning from results. It is a way to make the controllable parts of a draft more deliberate.

Why scoring cannot replace analytics

The reverse mistake is just as limiting. A good Pinello score does not tell you whether the audience saw the Pin, clicked through, saved it, or completed an action on your site. It does not know your account’s full distribution history or the business result attached to a visit.

That is why Pinello avoids calling its score a reach score or a ranking score. It works with the fields it can inspect and reports a quality signal. If you need account-level results, use Pinterest’s own reporting and your site’s analytics.

Give each measurement the right time window

The timing of the check changes what you can reasonably conclude. A score can be useful immediately because its job is to catch an editable issue. Analytics usually needs a defined period before it becomes useful evidence. A Pin published yesterday may not have enough activity to compare fairly with a Pin that has been available for months.

Choose a review window that matches the question. If you are checking whether a destination receives traffic, look at outbound clicks alongside the same period in your site analytics. If you are comparing creative approaches, keep the topic, audience, and publishing context in mind. Avoid turning a short-lived spike into a permanent rule.

This is also why a spreadsheet of raw counts can mislead. Record the date range, the Pin, the goal, and any obvious context. A measured result becomes more useful when you can explain what was actually measured.

Three ways to combine the tools

For occasional creators: Use a short Pinello check before publishing. After a meaningful batch of Pins, review Analytics rather than obsessing over each individual result.

For shop owners and bloggers: Keep a simple record of the Pin topic, destination, and the metric that matters. Use Pinello to improve consistency before publishing, then compare your own Pins over a sensible period.

For teams: Agree on a pre-publish standard and a post-publish reporting standard. The first can cover the five Pinello dimensions. The second can define which Pinterest metrics and business outcomes the team will review. Keeping the two checklists separate makes meetings less confusing.

The honest answer

Use scoring to improve what is still editable. Use Analytics to measure what happened after distribution. Use neither as a promise about what Pinterest will do next.

If you want a pre-publish second set of eyes inside Pinterest, try Pinello. It is designed to help you fix the draft you control, then leave the measurement of published results to Pinterest Analytics and your own reporting.
