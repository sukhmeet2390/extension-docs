Why hashtags no longer work on Pinterest

If you learned Pinterest marketing a few years ago, you were probably told to stuff a few hashtags at the end of every description.

That advice is outdated.

Pinterest deprecated hashtags in 2023, which means they no longer function as a meaningful discovery tool on the platform. So if you are still adding hashtag bundles to your descriptions, you are using space that could be doing better work.

What to do instead:

  • Put your primary keyword in the first sentence
  • Add 1-2 closely related phrases naturally in the description
  • Match the keyword theme across the image, title, description, board, and landing page
  • Write for humans first, search clarity second

In short: stop thinking in hashtags and start thinking in keyword alignment.

Before vs. after: a quick optimization example

Here is what a weak pin draft can look like before optimization.

Before

  • Title: “Cute idea”
  • Description: “Love this so much!! #decor #home #farmhouse”
  • Board: “Home”
  • Image: Beautiful, but no text overlay
  • URL: Homepage instead of the actual tutorial

Why it underperforms:

  • The title says nothing searchable
  • The description wastes the first sentence
  • Hashtags do not help discovery anymore
  • The board is too broad
  • The destination is not specific

After

  • Title: “Farmhouse Pantry Organization Ideas for Small Kitchens”
  • Description: “Farmhouse pantry organization ideas for small kitchens with labeled jars, shelf styling tips, and budget-friendly storage inspiration. Save for later.”
  • Board: “Small Kitchen Organization”
  • Image: Vertical 1000x1500 design with a bold text overlay
  • URL: Direct link to the pantry organization blog post

Same creator. Same topic. Much stronger search signal.

That is the goal of this checklist: not making your pins sound robotic, but making them unmistakably clear.
