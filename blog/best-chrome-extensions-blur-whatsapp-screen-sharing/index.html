Best Chrome Extensions to Blur WhatsApp During Screen Sharing

2026-11-15
#whatsapp#whatsapp web#screen sharing#privacy#chrome extensions

Screen sharing turns an ordinary WhatsApp Web tab into a privacy risk. A chat preview can expose a private message, a contact name can reveal who you are talking to, and a profile photo can identify someone before you notice what is visible in the shared window.

Privacy Blur for WhatsApp is a free Chrome extension that blurs chats, messages, names, and media on WhatsApp Web during screen sharing. It is designed for the practical problem: keep WhatsApp open, but decide which parts of the interface other people can see.

This guide compares Chrome extensions that add a visual privacy layer to WhatsApp Web. The useful differences are not just whether an extension can blur a message. Look at what it can hide, whether you can reveal one item without exposing everything, whether it includes a screen lock, and whether its privacy model is clear.

What a WhatsApp privacy extension should protect

WhatsApp Web exposes more than the open conversation. Depending on the page state, people nearby or viewers of a shared window may see:

  • The active contact or group name
  • Chat names in the left sidebar
  • Last-message previews
  • Profile photos and avatars
  • Message text and timestamps
  • Photos, videos, and other media
  • Text in the compose box
  • Status or About information

A useful extension lets you choose the level of protection. You may want to blur everything before a client call, or hide only message previews while working in a shared office. Hover reveal is also important: it lets you read one item locally without removing the blur from the rest of the page.

Chrome Web Store listings and features can change. Check the current listing, permissions, update date, and privacy disclosure before installing any extension that operates on WhatsApp Web.

1. Privacy Blur for WhatsApp — best for choosing exactly what stays private

Privacy Blur for WhatsApp is built around independent controls instead of one all-or-nothing blur. Its panel lets you choose messages, contact names, chat-list names, preview text, profile photos, the compose box, images and videos, and Status or About information.

That matters when your screen-sharing situation changes. Before a team meeting, you may enable every zone. During a support call, you may keep the chat visible but hide names and previews. When recording a tutorial, you may blur messages and media while leaving the interface readable.

The global toggle provides a fast way to switch the whole privacy layer on or off. Hover reveal lets you inspect a blurred item locally while the rest of the page stays protected. New WhatsApp content can continue to appear while the blur controller watches the changing page.

The free version covers the core blur zones and hover reveal. Pro adds settings persistence, keyboard shortcuts, keyword rules, and sender rules. A seven-day trial is available, followed by a one-time $5 lifetime upgrade. The product page explains that WhatsApp chat content, contact names, and media are not sent to Amrita Labs; blur logic runs locally in the browser. Anonymous product analytics and billing events are separate from message content.

Choose this option if your main problem is not only “blur WhatsApp,” but “blur the right parts of WhatsApp for this particular call.”

2. Blur for WhatsApp Web — best for broad blur plus a PIN lock

Blur for WhatsApp Web focuses on hiding names, profile pictures, messages, and other sensitive content. Its Chrome Web Store description also highlights an optional PIN lock for the WhatsApp screen.

That combination fits users who want a straightforward privacy mode and a stronger barrier when they step away from the computer. It is less about building a detailed workflow around separate zones and more about quickly hiding the information most likely to be exposed in a shared room or meeting.

Check whether its current controls match the parts of WhatsApp you need to hide. If you need separate switches for chat-list previews, media, the compose box, and status information, compare those controls directly in the current listing and extension UI.

3. WhatsApp Web Privacy Blur — best for a focused WhatsApp-only blur layer

WhatsApp Web Privacy Blur is another focused option for hiding chats, names, photos, previews, and media. Its listing describes hover reveal, a password lock, local browser controls, and no account requirement.

This type of extension works well for someone who wants a small privacy layer without changing how WhatsApp works. The important check is whether the extension still receives updates as WhatsApp Web changes. Read the listing’s update information and recent reviews, then test the exact screens you use before relying on it for a live presentation.

As with any browser extension that runs over private conversations, treat the privacy disclosure as part of the feature set. “Blur” describes what viewers see; it does not automatically tell you what the extension can access or transmit.

4. WhatsApp Privacy - Blur & Lock — best for a lock-oriented workflow

WhatsApp Privacy - Blur & Lock combines visual blur with a local screen-lock concept. Its listing describes controls for messages, previews, names, profile pictures, media, and the message input area, along with a password lock for the page.

This is useful if your concern continues after the meeting ends. Blur reduces accidental exposure while the tab is visible; a lock can help when you leave the workstation. Those are different jobs, so confirm whether the lock behavior, inactivity handling, and recovery flow fit your setup.

Avoid treating a browser lock as encryption. It is a visual and access-control layer inside the browser. It does not protect WhatsApp data from browser developer tools, malware, or someone who can access the underlying account.

How to choose between them

Start with the exact privacy problem:

You need selective controls

Choose an extension with separate zones if you want to hide chat names but keep the interface readable, or hide message previews without covering the entire page. Selective controls reduce the temptation to turn privacy protection off because the whole page became unusable.

You need a quick all-page mode

A broad blur toggle is useful immediately before a meeting or recording. Test it on the active chat, sidebar, profile photo, and media viewer. A control that covers only the open message area may leave identifying information elsewhere on the page.

You need to inspect one message privately

Look for hover reveal and test it with the cursor. Anyone viewing your shared screen can see the revealed area, so stop sharing before revealing private content. Do not assume every extension uses the same reveal behavior.

You leave WhatsApp open in shared spaces

A screen lock can complement blur. Use it when you step away from a laptop, work in a coworking space, or switch from a private task to a public presentation. Confirm whether the lock is local, how it is triggered, and what happens after reload.

You care about message privacy

Read the extension’s privacy disclosure before installing. Prefer clear explanations of permissions, local processing, analytics, billing, and data retention. A privacy tool should not require vague trust when it operates over private conversations.

A safe screen-sharing routine

Before sharing your screen:

  1. Open WhatsApp Web and wait for the page to finish loading.
  2. Enable the zones you need to hide.
  3. Check the active chat, chat list, header, profile photo, media, and compose box.
  4. Turn on the global blur mode if you do not want to inspect each zone separately.
  5. Start sharing only the intended browser tab or window.
  6. If you need to read one message, use hover reveal rather than disabling all blur.
  7. Turn the privacy layer back on before switching chats.
  8. Stop sharing before changing privacy settings or opening unrelated private pages.

The extension reduces accidental exposure, but it does not replace careful screen-sharing habits. Do not claim that a blur extension encrypts WhatsApp, hides content from browser developer tools, or protects a different tab, desktop app, or device.

Final recommendation

For a simple blur-and-lock workflow, compare the lock-focused extensions first. For a configurable screen-sharing workflow, Privacy Blur for WhatsApp is the strongest fit when you want independent zones, hover reveal, and a fast global toggle on WhatsApp Web.

The right choice is the one you can verify on the exact WhatsApp screens you share. Test the sidebar, active conversation, previews, media, profile photos, and compose box before your next meeting. A privacy extension earns its place when it makes that check quick and repeatable.
